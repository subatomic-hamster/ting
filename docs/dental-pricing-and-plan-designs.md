# Ground Ting's dental estimates in licensable data

*Research synthesized October 4, 2026. Every figure carries the date of its source. Dollar amounts labeled "demo" or "illustrative" are derived estimates, not quotes, contracted fees or Lincoln rates.*

**The only source that legitimately gives Ting all three price types it needs (billed charges, in-network allowed amounts and out-of-network UCR percentiles) by CDT code for Greensboro's ZIP3 274 is a paid FAIR Health license: its FH Charge Dental and FH Allowed Dental modules, or its API.** FAIR Health doesn't publish prices for these, so a quote is required. The free consumer site that was previously scraped explicitly forbids both scraping and commercial use. For the demo, the team can stop waiting on a vendor. A defensible Greensboro price table can be built this week from public government data: the North Carolina Medicaid dental fee schedule, scaled up by the ADA Health Policy Institute's 2024 North Carolina ratios of Medicaid to private-insurance payments and of Medicaid to dentist charges. Each figure should be cross-checked against national consumer averages and labeled "illustrative." In parallel, the team should ask for Context4's free 10-code evaluation and FAIR Health's research pricing. For production, the most defensible source is Lincoln's own: its DentalConnect PPO fee schedules and U&C tables, which are what Lincoln actually uses to pay claims, or a FAIR Health license, plus an ADA commercial CDT license in either case. On plan design, real Lincoln employer Low/High pairs, including one from Iredell County, NC, share a common base: a $50/$150 deductible waived for preventive care and 100/80/50 coinsurance. The Low and High options differ mainly in annual maximum, orthodontia, out-of-network basis and, in some pairs, whether endodontics and oral surgery count as Basic or Major. The recommended replacements for "Acme Dental Low/High" are therefore a **$1,000-maximum Low option with no ortho and MAC-based out-of-network pay** and a **$2,000-maximum High option with $1,500 child ortho, 90th-percentile U&C and endo/oral surgery moved to Basic**. Illustrative employee-only premiums are about $30 and $46 a month.

## FAIR Health is the only licensable source of allowed amounts by CDT and geozip

FAIR Health sells two dental benchmark modules. **FH Charge Dental** covers "billed charges arrayed by Current Dental Terminology (CDT®) codes," and **FH Allowed Dental** covers "amounts allowed by plans for dental procedures" ([FAIR Health Benchmark Modules](https://www.fairhealth.org/benchmark-data-products/benchmark-modules)). Its API, described in a May 1, 2017 article, delivers **charge and allowed benchmarks from the 5th to 95th percentile across 493 geozips**, built on twelve months of data, refreshed twice a year, and covering more than 15,000 procedure codes including dental. FAIR Health pitches the API explicitly at "insurers' member portals" and enrollment platforms ([FAIR Health API article](https://www.fairhealth.org/article/easy-access-to-healthcare-cost-estimates-through-web-applications)). Geozips roughly follow the first three digits of a ZIP code ([FAIR Health FAQs](https://www.fairhealth.org/faqs)), so ZIP 27401 resolves to geozip 274. Organizations can also private-label the whole FAIR Health Consumer site, including its dental cost estimator, under their own brand ([FAIR Health licensing article](https://www.fairhealth.org/article/enhanced-consumer-resources-available-for-licensing-and-private-labeling)). **None of these products has a published price.** Every page sends buyers to service@fairhealth.org or 855-301-3247. The research program, however, prices funded projects "at cost" and can provide in-kind data to unfunded projects under a standard Data Use Agreement ([FAIR Health Research](https://www.fairhealth.org/who-we-serve/research)). That makes a sample extract for geozip 274 a realistic request for a demo team, although the agreement would probably restrict public display. FH Allowed Dental is the only product the research turned up that directly supplies in-network allowed amounts by CDT code and geography, and those are the numbers an in-network estimator most needs.

Two FAIR Health channels are contractually off-limits. The consumer site's Terms of Use (last updated February 22, 2023) limit it to "personal, consumer use," bar any "robot, spider... or other manual or automatic device" used to "scrape" or "data mine," and prohibit commercial exploitation ([FAIR Health Consumer Terms](https://www.fairhealthconsumer.org/terms-of-use)). The inexpensive **FH Fee Estimator** may be licensed only by practices with "fewer than 5 licensed professionals," and its license excludes insurers, payors and consultants ([FH Fee Estimator FAQ](https://www.feeestimator.org/faq)). Optum resells FAIR Health charge data as its **2026 Dental Customized Fee Analyzer**, which offers seven percentiles from the 50th to the 95th by geozip. Optum doesn't publish the price or the license terms, and the product contains charges only, not allowed amounts ([Optum Coding](https://www.optumcoding.com/product/63218/reference-products/physician-fees/)).

The other commercial options are cheaper but each has a gap. **Context4 Healthcare** comes closest to FAIR Health. Its 2025 brochure describes a dental module with 25th to 95th percentiles across "almost 500 geo-zip regions," updated semiannually, with pricing "based on your claim volume or your memberships." It also offers a **free evaluation**: send 10 codes and three ZIPs and Context4 returns the 25th, 50th and 95th percentiles ([Context4 2025 brochure](https://www.context4healthcare.com/wp-content/uploads/2025-ucr-fee-data.pdf)). Its data comes from claims "before the claims are ever touched by the payer," so it reflects billed charges, not allowed amounts. Wasserman's **National Dental Advisory Service** is the cheapest legitimate dataset. The 2026 Book & Software costs **$259 on sale (list $358)** ([Wasserman NDAS 2026](https://wasserman-medical.com/shop/product/ndas-2026-book-software/)). The 2025 book costs $149 and provides 40th to 95th percentiles plus ZIP3 geographic multipliers ([Wasserman NDAS 2025](https://wasserman-medical.com/shop/product/national-dental-advisory-service-2024-fee-report-book-25/)). The software, however, ships only under a signed license, and nothing in the research confirmed redistribution or display rights. Sikka's Fee Survey ($495 per PDF report, covering three ZIPs, as of October 2026) is sold to practitioners and states no display license ([Sikka Fee Survey](https://www.sikka.ai/fee-survey)).

Three commonly assumed sources turn out to be dead ends. The **ADA Survey of Dental Fees was discontinued in 2023** and pulled from download "due to a change in law eliminating safe harbor disclosure," and the ADA says it is "forbidden by federal law to set or recommend fees" ([ADA HPI](https://www.ada.org/resources/research/health-policy-institute/dental-care-market)). Blog posts that cite an "ADA 2023 Dental Fees Survey" should therefore be treated as unreliable. **Transparency in Coverage files exclude stand-alone dental**: the November 12, 2020 final rule exempts excepted benefits, and limited-scope dental that members can opt out of counts as excepted even when it is sold alongside medical coverage ([TiC final rule, 85 FR 72158](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2020-11-12/html/2020-24591.htm)). That rules out Turquoise-style datasets that are built from those files. North Carolina also has no operational all-payer claims database. As of 2025 it had only begun a Medicaid-only "first step toward a Payer Claims Database" ([NC HIEA, June 29, 2026](https://hiea.nc.gov/blog/2026/06/29/nc-health-information-exchange-authority-releases-inaugural-annual-report)).

Lincoln already runs a member-facing estimator through its DentalConnect partner go2dental.com. It returns "a range of costs for dental procedures in your ZIP code area" by CDT code or keyword but doesn't calculate plan payment ([go2dental Lincoln estimator](https://clients.go2dental.com/lfg/GetFee); page returned HTTP 403 when fetched, so this is from search snippets). The research couldn't identify which data vendor supplies it. Lincoln's 2020 product flier says the DentalConnect networks "are comprised of several leased" networks. It also says out-of-network claims are reimbursed at **U&C at the 50th, 70th, 80th, 90th or 95th percentile, or at MAC** ([Lincoln product flier, ©2020](https://www.lincolnfinancial.com/pbl-static/pdf/GP-IP---what-we-offer---callout5---flier-PDF.pdf)). That method lines up directly with percentile-based charge benchmarks.

## Public government data supports a defensible Greensboro demo table today

North Carolina Medicaid publishes a full CDT-coded dental fee schedule as free PDF and XLSX files ([NC Medicaid fee schedule archive](https://medicaid.ncdhhs.gov/providers/fee-schedules/dental-fee-schedules-archive)). The rates are essentially frozen: "State Medicaid payment rates for dentists haven't changed since 2008" ([NC Newsline, April 21, 2026](https://ncnewsline.com/2026/04/21/nc-dentists-begin-public-push-for-increased-medicaid-payments/)). A 3% cut on October 1, 2025 was reversed on December 10, 2025 ([NC Governor, Dec. 10, 2025](https://governor.nc.gov/news/press-releases/2025/12/10/governor-stein-continues-stand-3-million-medicaid-patients-north-carolina-directs-ncdhhs-restore)). The ADA-hosted 2025 copy lists the same values effective January 1, 2022 ([ADA-hosted NC Medicaid 2025 schedule](https://www.ada.org/-/media/project/ada-organization/ada/ada-org/files/advocacy/medicaid/medicaid-fee-schedule/medicaid_fee_schedule_2025_nc.pdf?rev=1cc4b096d3014d1283082f41893e46ab&hash=51F5A88417AAB9E3C5913232D63333D6)). The ADA Health Policy Institute's 2024 workbook, published October 2024 and built on FAIR Health charges and DentaBase private allowed amounts from April 2023 to March 2024, puts **North Carolina adult Medicaid fee-for-service at 54.8% of private dental insurance payments and 36.3% of average dentist charges** ([ADA HPI 2024 XLSX](https://www.ada.org/-/media/project/ada-organization/ada/ada-org/files/resources/research/hpi/HPIData_Medicaid_Reimbursement_2024.xlsx)). Dividing each Medicaid rate by those ratios gives an in-network allowed proxy and a billed-charge proxy for each code. The method also implies an **allowed-to-charge ratio of about 0.66, a network discount of about 34%**. That figure is consistent with the NC Flex 2026 guide's statement that in-network fees are "typically 30-45% less than the average charges in the same area" ([NC Flex 2026 Dental guide](https://oshr.nc.gov/2026-ncflex-dental-plan-details-benefits-guide/open)) and with a national carrier's actuarially backed claim of "more than 35%" average savings.[^1]

The table below applies that method to Ting's nine demo codes. It needs an out-of-network 90th-percentile column, and no public source gives percentiles, so the 90th percentile is set at **1.25 times the median charge**. That multiplier is the average ratio of 90th percentile to median in the only published percentile pairs found: Sikka's 2015 data, published February 8, 2016, which shows D0120 at $48 vs. $62, D1110 at $91 vs. $115, D2740 at $1,131 vs. $1,390 and D2950 at $270 vs. $335 ([Dental Products Report](https://www.dentalproductsreport.com/view/latest-fee-data-18-popular-procedure-codes)). D2740 doesn't appear on the NC Medicaid schedule, and the Medicaid-derived D8080 figure is implausibly low. For those two codes, the billed proxy instead starts from CareCredit's 2023–2024 national averages ($1,399 for a porcelain crown and $6,343 for metal braces), scaled by CareCredit's North Carolina-to-national exam ratio ($198/$203) ([CareCredit Dentist Prices](https://www.carecredit.com/dentistry/costs/)), and the 0.66 ratio is then applied to get allowed amounts.

The cross-checks mostly agree, and the places where they don't are useful. Greensboro practices sell in-office membership plans that bundle two cleanings, exams and X-rays for **$396 to $549 a year** (October 2026 listings, e.g. [Greensboro Dental](https://www.greensborodental.com/), [Pleasant Dental](https://pleasantdentalgreensboro.membershipenrollment.com/), [Premier Dental Greensboro](https://premierdentalgreensboro.com/dental-savings-plan/)). The proxy prices that same bundle at about $460, which sits inside that range. Two figures run high: the proxies for bitewings and the two-surface composite exceed CareCredit's national averages by roughly 35–40%. The HPI ratio is an index weighted across a 13-code adult basket that includes both codes, so the error is a property of the index, not a sourcing mistake. The demo should show these numbers as ranges rather than precise point prices. The NY workers' compensation schedule is not a reliable ceiling either: its $81 maximum for D1110 sits below Sikka's ten-year-old $91 median, so it works only as a rough comparison point. Codes outside the HPI basket (D2740, D2950, D3330, D8080) carry the most uncertainty, and D8080 is the weakest number in the table. The HPI ratios themselves have changed between editions: secondary sources quote earlier North Carolina figures such as 52.9% that don't match the 2024 workbook, so the team should pin the 2024 vintage in its methodology note. Other public schedules are useful only for scenarios, not as price sources. The South Carolina State Health Plan's 2025 "Basic Dental" allowances (D1110 $30.10, D3330 $339.80) ([SC Exhibit 10](https://procurement.sc.gov/files/Exhibit_10_--_2025_Basic_Dental_Fee_Schedule.pdf)) and NYSHIP's out-of-network schedule effective October 1, 2024 (D1110 $40) ([NYSHIP PDF](https://www.cs.ny.gov/employee-benefits/nyship/shared/publications/out-of-network/2025/dental-oon-reimbursement-10012024.pdf)) both show how far a low plan allowance can sit below a dentist's bill. That makes them good material for an illustrative balance-billing demo, but they aren't UCR percentiles. Discount-plan and carrier fee schedules that circulate as PDFs are competitor-branded and have no stated reuse terms. They must never appear in the app.[^2]

## Ship public proxies now, then license FAIR Health or use Lincoln's own fees

**For the demo now**, the team should load the table above, label every dollar figure as "illustrative estimate for the Greensboro area (ZIP3 274), derived from public NC Medicaid rates and ADA HPI 2024 ratios; not a quote," and show ranges, using the CareCredit cross-check as the low end where it falls below the proxy. Context4 is a reasonable second quote for charges, but it doesn't supply allowed amounts.

### The ADA CDT license applies whichever price source is chosen

The CDT code set is ADA intellectual property. The ADA states that "all use, copying or distribution of CDT, or any portion thereof... in any product or services... requires a valid commercial user license from the ADA" and that "redistribution of the CDT Codes alone is not permitted." Licenses are royalty-based, are requested through CDT-SNODENT@ada.org and take two to four weeks, sometimes up to two months ([ADA Licensing for Commercial Users](https://www.ada.org/publications/ada-store-products/licensing-for-commercial-users); [ADA News, May 2023](https://adanews.ada.org/ada-news/2023/may/cdt-is-ada-intellectual-property-source-of-revenue-for-association/)). FAIR Health's own terms note that CDT is licensed to it with restrictions ([FAIR Health Consumer Terms](https://www.fairhealthconsumer.org/terms-of-use)). For the demo, store code numbers alongside Ting's own plain-language labels, as in the table above, never the ADA's official descriptors. Add a notice that "CDT codes are © American Dental Association." Treat any commercial launch as requiring a license. This research couldn't settle whether bare code numbers alone require a license, so that question needs legal review rather than an assumption. The HPI workbook is likewise "© 2024 American Dental Association. All rights reserved," so the app should cite the ratios rather than redistribute the file.

Coinsurance is **100/80/50** (preventive/basic/major), or 100/85/50 at Iredell, and **child orthodontia is covered at 50%**. The pattern is clear. Coinsurance is often identical across the two options (Iredell, Prosper). The Low/High difference comes mostly from the **annual maximum** (Low typically $1,000–$1,500, High $2,000), from **orthodontia** (Low either excludes it or covers children only at a lower maximum), and from the **out-of-network basis**. The Low plans at Life School, Lake Worth and Prosper all pay out-of-network claims at MAC, while the High plans at Life School, Lake Worth and Celina pay at the "90th Percentile of Usual & Customary Allowance." Iredell's guide describes the same thing as "an average of 9 out of 10 dentist charges in your area." Life School and Lake Worth also use a lever the optimizer can show to members: the **High option moves endodontics, periodontics and oral surgery into Basic at 80%**, while Lincoln's default classification puts endodontics, periodontal surgery and oral surgery in Major ([Lincoln flier](https://www.lincolnfinancial.com/pbl-static/pdf/GP-IP---what-we-offer---callout5---flier-PDF.pdf)). Lincoln certificates are consistent on frequency limits. Celina's 2026 certificate allows two routine exams a year, up to three cleanings with no six-month spacing rule, two sets of bitewings, a full-mouth X-ray series once every three years, fluoride through age 18, sealants through age 15, crowns once per five years and filling replacement once per 24 months. It **explicitly covers composite fillings** with no amalgam downgrade, and it includes a least-costly-alternative clause and a missing-tooth exclusion ([Celina cert](https://docs.thebenefitshub.com/External.aspx?DocID=15588548&InBrowser=1)). Life School's Low certificate is tighter: two cleanings a year, a full-mouth series once every five years and fluoride through age 15 ([Life School Low cert](https://docs.thebenefitshub.com/External.aspx?DocID=5593117&InBrowser=1)).

MaxRewards, Lincoln's optional rollover feature, follows fixed tables tied to the annual maximum. At a **$1,000 maximum**, the claim threshold is $600, the rollover is $250 ($350 if only network dentists are used) and the account cap is $1,000. That table appears in Lincoln's 2022 MaxRewards flyer and in a North Carolina employer's plan ([MaxRewards flyer](https://d36ps6jii4qpju.cloudfront.net/files/23528ed6c5f0ad548a279c876b70076f-FILE.pdf?response-content-disposition=inline&response-content-type=application/pdf&version=1704868422); [East Carolina CDJR, NC](https://docs.pierceins.com/pdf/east-carolina-auto/EASTCARO-Dental-enroll-packet.pdf)). At a **$1,750 maximum**, the threshold is $800, the rollover $350 ($525 with network dentists only) and the cap $1,500 (Celina, 2026–27). Rollovers are deposited on the 65th day of the following year, require at least one claim during the year, are used only after the annual maximum is reached, and are forfeited after any break in coverage ([Celina cert](https://docs.thebenefitshub.com/External.aspx?DocID=15588548&InBrowser=1)). Two problems in the source documents should not be copied into the demo. Iredell's plan summary imposes a 12-month late-entrant wait on Basic services, while its benefits guide says Basic is covered "day one." Iredell's High plan also advertises adult ortho while carrying a boilerplate exclusion for appliances installed after age 19.

National and North Carolina benchmarks agree with the Lincoln designs. **73% of dental PPO enrollees had an annual maximum of $1,500 or more in 2024**, and only 3% had a deductible of $100 or more ([NADP, Nov. 25, 2025](https://www.nadp.org/new-data-sheds-light-on-dental-benefits-and-the-cost-of-serving-enrollees/)). Of in-network maximums, 32.8% fall between $1,000 and $1,500 and 48.2% between $1,500 and $2,500 ([ADA News, Dec. 19, 2025](https://adanews.ada.org/ada-news/2025/december/dear-ada-annual-maximums/)). The ADA's 2023 "typical" PPO has a $50 deductible, 100/80/50 coinsurance, a $1,500 maximum and 50% ortho up to $1,000 ([ADA primer](https://www.ada.org/-/media/project/ada-organization/ada/ada-org/files/resources/practice/dental-insurance/dental_benefits_an_introduction_final.pdf)). The average employer DPPO employee-only premium was **$28.70–$30.71 a month in 2024**, and premiums rose less than 1% that year ([NADP Understanding Dental Benefits](https://www.nadp.org/about-dental-plans-care/understanding-dental-benefits/); [NADP 2025 Premium Report](https://www.nadp.org/research/2025-dental-premium-report/)). Claims data show only 2.9% of DPPO group enrollees reached their maximum in 2024 (NADP), while 12% of insured adults *say* they did ([CareQuest, Feb. 2026](https://carequest.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/CareQuest_Institute_Maxed-Out_2.12.26.pdf)). The optimizer should treat hitting the maximum as uncommon but costly for heavy-restorative users. Three non-Lincoln North Carolina plans, carriers withheld from the app, work as benchmarks only. NC Flex 2026, the 100% employee-paid plan for state agencies and universities, offers High ($5,000 maximum, $58.76 employee-only), Classic ($1,500, $37.94) and Low ($1,000, 100/50/not covered, $25.64) ([NC Flex 2026 guide](https://oshr.nc.gov/2026-ncflex-dental-plan-details-benefits-guide/open)). Guilford County's 2025 plans run 100/80/50 with $500 and $2,000 maximums and $2,000 adult-and-child ortho ([Guilford County 2025 guide](https://www.guilfordcountync.gov/2025-2026-benefits-guide/open)). Guilford County Schools' 2026 plans also run 100/80/50 with $1,500 and $2,000 maximums and $1,500 ortho ([GCS PPO cert](https://d12vgt9cnuri7d.cloudfront.net/wp-content/uploads/sites/142/2026/02/09131847/010350599C05_B00020251_ECERT.pdf)). NC Flex's $5,000 maximum is a national outlier and should not anchor the High option.

These parameters produce meaningful differences on the demo codes. Take a molar root canal (D3330) at a network dentist, using the demo allowed amount of $782. On the High option it is Basic: the plan pays $585.60 and the member pays $196.40 (the $50 deductible plus 20% of the rest). On the Low option it is Major, and the member pays $416. At an out-of-network dentist billing the $1,181 median, the gap widens. High's 90th-percentile U&C ($1,476) exceeds the bill, so the member pays only about $276. Low's MAC caps the allowed amount at $782, so the member pays about $815 once the balance bill is included. A crown (D2740, $904 allowed) costs the member about $477 on either option. For crowns, the deciding factors are the annual maximum and MaxRewards, not coinsurance. These worked numbers are illustrative, and they show exactly the trade-offs the optimizer exists to surface.

## Conclusion

Rebuilding the data around how Lincoln actually pays claims pays off twice. Lincoln reimburses out-of-network care at a chosen U&C percentile or at MAC, and its Low and High plans differ mainly on that basis, on maximums and on class placement. The demo's most persuasive comparisons (balance-bill exposure, root canal cost, orthodontia) therefore depend on getting the *ratio* between billed, allowed and 90th-percentile amounts right more than on any single absolute price. The public Medicaid-plus-HPI method gets those ratios defensibly right today, while the absolute levels remain open to correction, especially for crowns and orthodontia. Production accuracy, in turn, is less a data-purchase problem than a partnership question. If Lincoln will share its DentalConnect fee schedules and U&C tables, Ting skips the FAIR Health negotiation entirely and produces estimates that match real explanation-of-benefits statements. The CDT license and display-rights terms are the non-negotiable items to resolve before anything ships.
